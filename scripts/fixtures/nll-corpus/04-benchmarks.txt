**What the DRAM counter shows (measured; the interpretation is inferred).** At the default (D=4, 14–16 threads) the memory controller moves ≈ 98 GB/s averaged over the whole C1 step and ≈ 106 GB/s at C4, against the 125 GB/s that this CPU's two CCDs can pull (E25/E27). The tier is streaming weights for at least 78 % of the step wall (98 / 125) — it is **bandwidth-bound at bs=1 already**, not core-bound: 12 threads lose 5–12 % *and* pull 5–7 % less bandwidth; 14 → 16 threads is flat in both. D=0 is the control that makes this legible: same bytes, but the counter drops to 64.5 GB/s at C1 because the CPU reads no longer overlap the GPU phase — deferral raises *achieved* bandwidth (64.5 → 98), it does not shrink the byte count. Caveat: the counter is socket-wide (includes GPU DMA of pinned buffers and Engram page-cache reads) and 1 s samples average ≈ 12–33 steps, so the per-step byte figures are ± 15 % and the ≥ 78 % duty figure is a lower bound.

Launcher defaults (slot 3 BIOS, patched image, spec off, D=4, 226/158 placement, chunk 4096, 300 W — never binds) plus `KT_B2_UVA_EXPERTS=S`: the last *S* CPU experts of every layer are also held pinned in host RAM in FlashInfer layout and computed by each rank's stock SM120 CUTLASS MXFP4 grouped GEMM over UVA, forked on a side stream beside kt's `submit()`, joined after `sync()`; `KT_B2_MAX_TOKENS=1` hands them back to kt for any forward wider than one token. 512 tokens, distinct prompts, seed 4242, 6 runs, C1 fixture offset 6 / C4 offset 0. Microbench (`scripts/bench-b2-uva-moe.py`, `uva-moe-*.json`): UVA grouped GEMM is bit-equal to the VRAM run, graph-replayable, ≈ 0.09 ms floor + 0.21 ms per expert hit at TP2 (39–41 GiB/s effective per x16 link; plain memcpy 51–53), and unchanged under a concurrent 130 GB/s host-read hog once graphed; forked with the kt tier the step costs max(tier, B2), not the sum. kt's own read of one full expert costs ≈ 0.146 ms, so the PCIe reader is 1.4× slower per expert than the path it relieves.

What the numbers say:

- **Additive bandwidth is not additive throughput.** E4b's 98 % additivity (CCD reads + PCIe DMA) held — the HSMP DRAM counter rose as expected — but the step got no shorter because (a) each streamed expert costs more link time than kt's fabric time for it, and (b) D=4 deferral already hides most of the CPU tier's wall time behind the next layer (E21), so bytes taken off the CPU path were mostly bytes that were not on the critical path. E28 gained the full byte reduction because VRAM is free per expert; B2 pays 1.4×.
- **S = 60 makes B2 the longest pole**: 7.3 ms of link-bound GEMM per step on the side stream exceeds what it overlaps and C1 loses 3 %.
- **The token gate is not optional**: without it a 13-token prefill layer hits most of the 40 streamed experts and TTFT goes 0.61 → 2.5 s.
- 0 NaN / Xid / Traceback in all three servers; EDAC 0/0; GPU peak 148–171 W.

Verdict: **rejected as a decode lever; FEASIBILITY's 71–78 tok/s B2 column struck.** Code kept off by default (overlay `7e41317e`); the per-device fork/join stream pattern is reused for T3.20 Engram staging.

What the numbers say:

- **KV is not the binder.** "Memory pool end" reads 4.58 → 4.48 → 4.15 GB free for pools of 81,920 → 147,456 → 278,528 tokens: DSV4's compressed KV costs ≈ 0.1–0.2 GB per 65K tokens per rank. Hypothesis (1) of E33 held; a 1M single-slot pool would fit in the ≈ 4 GB left after capture.
- **The binder is the DSV4 sparse-attention indexer's dense prefill scoring** (verified in the overlay source, upstream code): `_low_ratio_index_topk_dense` builds fp32 logits `[chunk_tokens, lc]` with `lc` = prefix length (compress ratio 1), i.e. 64 MiB per 4K of prefix per 4096-token chunk — 1 GiB at 64K, 2 GiB at 128K, 4 GiB at 262K, 16 GiB at 1M — and `select_candidate_blocks` pads a copy of it in slices bounded by `_TORCH_INDEXER_SCORE_BUDGET_BYTES = 1 << 30` (`deepseek_v4_backend.py:336`). The allocator-retry sizes (604, 738, 805, 872, 939, 1,007, 1,074 MiB) are exactly 4096 × prefix × 4 B at each chunk. Both OOMs are the `F.pad` inside `select_candidate_blocks`, Python-level, no Xid.
- **64K prefill is slower than 32K for the same reason**: ≈ 734–968 tok/s derived from TTFT at 55–62K against 845 measured at 32K; the dense score and the sparse-attention cost both grow with the prefix.
- **The deployment profile sits on the same edge.** E28's worst case (4 × 30K, min free 779 MiB) and E29's chunk-2048 reading (min free 2,923 MiB, ≈ 1.5 GB more, at −40 % prefill) are this transient at 32K; chunk size halves the logits, which is why chunk 2048 bought margin.
- **The ladder stops here, not at a placement**: 262K / 512K / 1M were not launched. At 226 / 158 the fix is in the scorer, not in the expert split (E34c's trade of ≈ 12 experts for 262K (0.34 GB per expert per rank, E28) costs ≈ −7 % C4 / −4 % C1 by the E28 byte model and still leaves 1M out of reach).
